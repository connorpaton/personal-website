We know how Nike turns out. That's what makes it easy to forget that Phil Knight didn't get to build it with the ending in front of him.

That's the part of Shoe Dog I care about. Not the finished company. The uncertainty before any of it looked inevitable.

## Building Without the Ending

Startup stories can get cleaned up after the fact. The doubts become lessons. The risks become obvious turning points. Everything points toward the outcome we already know.

What I appreciate here is the messiness. Making decisions with incomplete information. Continuing through setbacks. Trying to build something meaningful without a guarantee that the effort will be enough.

That feels closer to building than a neat list of principles does.

It also isn't proof that persistence always wins. We're reading the story of a company that made it. I want to take encouragement from that without pretending determination removes the risk.

## The People Beside You

The team piece matters just as much to me.

A shared purpose gives people a reason to care beyond being told what to do. And if you're bringing talented people into something uncertain, they need room to contribute more than obedience.

That's the kind of building I find exciting. People with different strengths, invested in the same thing, surprising each other with what they can do.

Shoe Dog is worth reading for the uncertainty and the relationships, not just the outcome. A reminder that a company can look impressive from the outside while the people inside are still figuring it out.
