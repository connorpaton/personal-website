It's easy to make getting started feel more complicated than it needs to be.

A perfect program. The right equipment. A complete understanding of everything before the first session.

I'd rather start with the basics: learn the movements, track the work, and leave room to recover. There's enough to pay attention to there.

## Progress Without Rushing It

Progressive overload means gradually asking more of yourself through weight, reps, or training frequency.

The word I'd emphasize is gradually.

Technique comes before adding weight. If the movement isn't there yet, scale back. Track what you're doing so progress is something you can look at, not just a feeling after a hard session.

Recovery belongs in that process too. It isn't an optional extra once you've done the work.

## A Simple Week

Here's one basic schedule to start from, not a requirement to organize your life around:

- Monday: Upper Body Push
- Wednesday: Lower Body
- Friday: Upper Body Pull
- Weekend: Rest and Recovery

I like the simplicity of having a plan you can understand. You know what the next session is. You also know when you're not training.

## Remember the Food

Training is only part of this. Food needs to support the work.

The nutrition basics in this outline are:

- Protein: 1.6-2.2g per kg of body weight
- Carbs: Fuel for workouts
- Fats: Hormonal health

This is a general starting point, not a personalized nutrition plan.

## Start Where You Are

Start with bodyweight exercises to build a foundation, then gradually add weight as your form improves.

I don't think this needs a more dramatic conclusion than that. Learn the movements. Keep track. Recover. Give yourself a chance to build on the work instead of trying to do everything at once.
